Empathy Map

Step into one person's shoes. Fill in what they say, think, do and feel, plus their pains and gains, from your interviews and observations. Then turn the gaps you notice into one insight.

    STEP 1 · 5 MIN

    Pick one person

    One real person you interviewed or watched, not “users”. Give them a name, one line about them and the goal they're trying to get done.

    STEP 2 · 15 MIN

    Fill the map

    Add notes to each section from your research: their exact words in Says, guesses about their mind in Thinks, what you saw in Does, emotions in Feels. Then their pains and gains.

    STEP 3 · 10 MIN

    Find the insight

    Look for contradictions, like saying “It's fine” while feeling anxious. Write one insight, then open it in the POV Statement tool and save the A4 sheet.

    Where the empathy map comes from

    The empathy map was created by Dave Gray, founder of the visual-thinking company XPLANE. It grew out of the caricature-style “big head” drawings that XPLANE's creative director Scott Matthews made in the mid-2000s, and Gray refined it into the empathy map published in Gamestorming (2010), the facilitation playbook he wrote with Sunni Brown and James Macanufo.

    The idea is simple: put one person in the middle and look at them from every side, what they say, think, do and feel, so the team designs for a real human and not an average “user”. The four-quadrant Says / Thinks / Does / Feels layout used here is the one taught by Nielsen Norman Group. Gray later updated his canvas with See, Hear, Pains and Gains; this tool keeps Pains and Gains too.

    Where your map is kept

    Your map stays in this browser and is never uploaded. Use Save A4 sheet or Copy as text to keep a copy.
