      STEP 1 · 3 MIN

      Name it

      One line: what it is and who it's for. Not sure yet? Write a POV statement first.

      STEP 2 · 15 MIN

      Fill 1 to 9

      Start with customers, end with costs. One idea per note, and no discussion yet.

      STEP 3 · 10 MIN

      Read and tidy

      Read every note aloud. Merge duplicates and drag notes into the right block.

      STEP 4 · 5 MIN

      Dot vote

      Everyone gets three dots for the strongest or riskiest ideas. Tap a note's dot button to add one.

      STEP 5 · 12 MIN

      Fit check

      Work through the warnings on the right, then save the A4 sheet and compare it with other teams.

      Where the Business Model Canvas comes from

      The Business Model Canvas was created by Swiss business theorist Alexander Osterwalder, building on his PhD research with Yves Pigneur at the University of Lausanne. It became known worldwide through their 2010 book Business Model Generation, which was co-created with 470 practitioners from 45 countries. Osterwalder's company, Strategyzer, publishes the canvas under a Creative Commons licence, which is why so many free versions of it exist.

      The canvas breaks any business into nine building blocks: who it serves, what it offers them, how it reaches them, how it keeps them, how it earns money, and what it needs, does, partners with and spends to make that happen. The right side is about customers and value; the left side is about running the business. A good canvas is one where every note on the left supports something on the right.

      Google lists the canvas as a method in the Define phase of its free Design Sprint Kit. The sticky notes, dot vote, fit check and A4 sheet on this page are made by Invent + Discover. Your canvas stays in your browser; nothing is uploaded.
