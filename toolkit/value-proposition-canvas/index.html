    What is the Value Proposition Canvas?

    The Value Proposition Canvas is a strategic management tool developed by Dr. Alexander Osterwalder (available via Strategyzer). It ensures that a product or service is explicitly aligned with what target customers actually value and need.

    The canvas acts as a detailed zoom-in on two building blocks of the broader Business Model Canvas: the Customer Profile (the circle) and the Value Map (the square).

    “Fit is a claim until customers confirm it.”

    Strategyzer, The Value Proposition Canvas. Linking a reliever to a pain on this canvas is a guess about fit: test it with real customers.
    STEP 1 · 10 MIN

    Start with the customer

    Fill the circle first. Customer jobs: what are they trying to get done? Pains: what annoys them or gets in the way? Gains: what outcomes or benefits do they want?

    STEP 2 · 10 MIN

    Map your value

    Fill the square. Products & services: what you offer. Pain relievers: how it removes specific pains. Gain creators: how it produces specific gains.

    STEP 3 · 10 MIN

    Match for fit

    Each fix sits on the same row as the pain or gain it answers, with a spark ✦ in the middle. A dark dot ● is a gap: use its dashed slot to add a fix. A ? is a fix that answers nothing listed: pick what it answers, or ask if it's needed.

    Where it comes from

    The Value Proposition Canvas was created by Alexander Osterwalder and is published by Strategyzer. It is set out in the book Value Proposition Design: How to Create Products and Services Customers Want (Wiley, 2014) by Alexander Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur, Gregory Bernarda, Alan Smith and Trish Papadakos.

    The Customer Profile describes one customer segment through their jobs, pains and gains. The Value Map lists what you offer and how it relieves those pains and creates those gains. You have fit when your value map answers what matters most in the profile.

    Strategyzer's canvas draws the Value Map as a square and the Customer Profile as a circle. Here the two halves meet in the middle like an open book, keeping the square edge and the round edge, so each fix sits on the same row as what it answers. Showing which reliever answers which pain follows the worked example by the Interaction Design Foundation (interaction-design.org).
