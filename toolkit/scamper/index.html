    Where SCAMPER comes from

    In 1953 the advertising executive Alex Osborn, who also gave us the word "brainstorming", published a checklist of questions for sparking ideas in his book Applied Imagination. In 1971 the educator Bob Eberle arranged those questions into the easy-to-remember word SCAMPER, in SCAMPER: Games for Imagination Development.

    The questions, examples and diagram on this page are written by Invent + Discover. How to use it:

    1. Pick one specific thing. "Our college canteen" works better than "food".
    2. Go letter by letter. Quantity first: judge later.
    3. Not every letter will fit every topic. Skip one if it's stuck, and come back.
    4. At the end, star the few ideas worth testing.
