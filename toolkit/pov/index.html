POV Statement

Say clearly who you're designing for, what they need, and why it matters. Four short steps.

Your point of view [ who ] needs a way to [ do something ] because [ the surprising reason ].

Who are you designing for?

One specific person or group. Add a detail that makes them real: age, situation, place.

What do they need to do?

Write it as an action, a verb. Not a product. You're describing the goal, not your idea for it.

needs a way to
A solution, not a needan app that alerts them
A needknow when to get off, without looking up

Why? What's the insight?

The reason behind the need, ideally something you noticed or heard that surprised you. This is what makes the problem worth solving.

because
Obviousit's important to them

    Where the POV statement comes from

    The Point of View (POV) statement belongs to the Define stage of design thinking, between understanding people (Empathize) and coming up with ideas (Ideate). This fill-in-the-blank version, [user] needs a way to [need] because [insight], is the POV Madlib taught by the Stanford d.school (Hasso Plattner Institute of Design) in its design thinking guides.

    A good POV is about one specific person, describes a need as an action rather than a product, and rests on an insight: something you learned that others might miss. From a good POV, “How might we…” questions almost write themselves, which is why this tool hands straight over to How Might We, and that hands over to Crazy 8s.
