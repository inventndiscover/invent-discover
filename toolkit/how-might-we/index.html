    Where “How might we” comes from

    How Might We is one of the best-known framing methods in design thinking. The phrasing is usually traced to Min Basadur, who used it in creative problem-solving work at Procter & Gamble in the 1970s. It later became a habit at IDEO and the Stanford d.school, and is now a standard step in Google's Design Sprint, where teams turn notes from experts into “How might we” sticky notes and vote on the best ones. Google teaches it in its free Design Sprint Kit.

    Each word does a job. How assumes there is an answer. Might says it's fine if an idea doesn't work. We makes it a shared problem. The lenses on this page (amp up the good, remove the bad, explore the opposite and the rest) follow the prompts design-thinking teachers commonly use to push a problem in different directions.
