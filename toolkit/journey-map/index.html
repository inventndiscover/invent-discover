User Journey Map

Follow one person through a task, stage by stage. Note where they meet you, what they do and think, how they feel along the way, and where it hurts. The lowest point on the curve is usually where to design first.

Definition from UX Design Institute. Layout after the classic customer journey map template. Credits

“A customer journey map (otherwise known as a user journey map) is a visual representation of how a user or customer interacts with your product. It maps out the steps they go through to complete a specific task or to achieve a particular goal, for example, purchasing a product from an e-commerce website or creating a profile on a dating app.”

Emily Stevens, How to design a customer journey map, UX Design Institute

    STEP 1 · 5 MIN

    Set the scene

    Pick one person and one task, and say what they expect. Name the stages they go through, from first noticing the need to the end. Use 3 to 7 stages.

    STEP 2 · 15 MIN

    Walk the journey

    For each stage, fill in the touchpoints, what they do and what they think, from your research. Then pick a face for how they feel. The curve draws itself.

    STEP 3 · 10 MIN

    Find the gaps

    Note the pain points, especially where the curve dips, and an opportunity for each one. Then save the A4 sheet.

    About journey maps

    A journey map tells the story of one person trying to get something done. Columns are the stages of that story; rows are the layers you want to see across it: where they meet the product (touchpoints), what they do and think, how they feel, and the pain points and opportunities that follow.

    The layout here follows the classic customer journey map template, with stage chevrons across the top and an emotion curve in the middle. We added a Thoughts row, a persona and scenario, and a coach that points to the lowest moment on the curve.

    Where your map is kept

    Your map stays in this browser and is never uploaded. Use Save A4 sheet or Copy as text to keep a copy.
