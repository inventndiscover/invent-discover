Jyotirlinga Compass

ज्योतिर्लिंग दिशा-सूचक

Choose one of the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines. An arrow turns to point the way from wherever you are standing, with the distance in kilometres.

Notice before you begin. This compass is for curiosity and learning. It is not for navigation and not for worship guidance. A phone compass is easily thrown off indoors and near metal, and the shrine positions here come from public encyclopaedia records, not from the temple trusts. Please do not use it to find your way or to orient yourself for prayer. Educational demo. Made for learning and research, not for commercial use.

Step 1 · Your location

Your location stays on your phone. It is read by the browser, used in this page to work out a direction and a distance, and never stored, never sent anywhere and never logged. Close the tab and it is gone.

Bearing — Compass —

No compass on this device, so the dial is held with north upwards and the arrow shows the bearing from true north. On a phone the arrow turns as you turn.

The damaru (डमरू) sounds while the trishul swings to a new direction, and you can tap it to hear it. When the trishul comes to rest, a pooja bell (घंटी) rings once. Both sounds are made by the page itself, not recordings: the damaru from two drum skins tuned a little apart, struck in turn by the beads on its cords; the bell from a bright brass note and its shimmering overtones. Switch them off with the button above.

दस दिशाएँ, the ten directions. The dial shows eight: the four main ones and the four corners (कोण): ईशान, आग्नेय, नैऋत्य and वायव्य. Tradition counts two more, ऊर्ध्व (upward) and अधो (downward), which a flat compass cannot show.

If the arrow wanders: wave the phone in a figure 8 a few times to calibrate it. Indoors, in a lift, in a car, or near a fridge, speakers, a laptop or steel furniture, the magnetic reading drifts and can be wrong by a long way. Step outside, away from metal, for a steadier reading.
